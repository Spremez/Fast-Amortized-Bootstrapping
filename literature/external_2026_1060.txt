Abstract. The Learning with Errors (LWE) problem serves as a cornerstone of modern cryptography, underlying advanced schemes such as Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE). Many FHE schemes adopt LWE instances with sparse ternary secrets, leaving them vulnerable to attacks. In 2022, Bi-Lu-Luo-Wang [ACISP 2022] proposed a hybrid dual attack that combines May's Meet-in-the-Middle (MITM) algorithm [Crypto 2021] with a dual attack and shows that it outperforms other attacks in a large range of FHE-type parameters. However, their attack suffers from two main efficiency bottlenecks: the costly enumeration of error entries and the large number of hash function labels. In this work, we conduct a systematic analysis of several variants of May's MITM algorithm equipped with different list constructions and hash functions. Based on this, we propose a new hybrid dual attack that incorporates the most efficient variant, effectively mitigating both bottlenecks. We further enhance the attack by adopting a better hypothesis testing algorithm for FHE settings. Addressing recent concerns raised by Ducas-Pulles [Crypto 2023] regarding the independence heuristic in dual attacks, we provide a rigorous theoretical and empirical analysis. We demonstrate that, for typical FHE parameters, our attack does not rely on the problematic independence heuristic and lies outside the contradictory regime. Finally, we compare our attack with previous hybrid attacks, showing consistent and significant improvements across all evaluated cases. In particular, our results invalidate the accelerated BGV scheme in [EUROCRYPT 2024] by reducing its bit-security below the claimed security level, with the most extreme case being 18 bits lower.

1 Introduction
The Learning with Errors (LWE) problem [39] has emerged as a foundational building block in lattice-based cryptography. The LWE problem asks to recovery

2

Bi et al.

a secret vector s given samples (A, b), where b = As + e mod q and e is a small error vector. A variety of cryptographic schemes, from homomorphic encryptions (HE), public key encryptions to digital signatures, base their security on LWE family of the lattice problems. LWE is widely believed to be quantum-safe and benefits from strong worst-case to average-case reductions, which means that it is at least as hard as some worst-case lattice problems. However, in practice, the bit-security of concrete LWE-based schemes depends crucially on parameter selection, making the precise evaluation of their security levels an essential task.
For efficiency reasons, many practical LWE-based schemes employ small and sparse secrets. As one of the most important implementation of LWE, the fully HE (FHE) schemes use sparse ternary secrets because the bootstrapping procedure, the key technique in FHE, relies on such sparsity for computational efficiency. However, the use of sparsity and small secrets makes this variant of LWE problem more vulnerable to attacks, and the bit-security of LWE with small/sparse secrets remains insufficiently understood [1]. Recently, the sparsesecret encapsulation technique [7] was proposed to improve the precision and efficiency of bootstrapping without compromising the security of the schemes. Since then lots of new proposed schemes have adopted sparse ternary secrets. Moreover, most widely used FHE libraries, including HEAAN, HElib, Lattigo, and PALISADE [18, 19, 27, 36], adopt sparse secrets, and the Homomorphic Encryption Standardization [22] has listed LWE with sparse secrets as an extension of the current document. These developments make it both important and urgent to rigorously assess the bit-security of LWE with small/sparse secrets.
Estimating the concrete bit-security of LWE-based schemes requires assessing their resilience against all known attacks. For FHE-type parameters, the two most powerful attack families are hybrid primal attacks [42, 20] and hybrid dual attacks [10, 5]. In this paper, we focus on the hybrid dual attack, which currently represents one of the most efficient approaches for FHE-type parameters. A hybrid dual attack partitions the secret vector into two parts: s = (s1, s2)  A2s2 + e = b - A1s1 mod q, then enumerates candidates for s1 while using short vectors in the dual lattice of A2 to perform hypothesis testing on the guesses. Currently, the most efficient hybrid dual attack on FHE is due to Bi-Lu-Luo-Wang [5], which incorporates May's Meet-LWE algorithm [31], an optimal Meet-in-the-Middle (MITM) method for solving sparse ternary LWE, to accelerate the enumeration of s1. However, embedding Meet-LWE into the hybrid dual framework amplifies its two inherent weakness with respect to errorguessing and hash labels. Consequently, although the Bi-Lu-Luo-Wang algorithm improves bit-security estimates in across a wide range of FHE parameters, it still faces two significant efficiency drawbacks: (1) the high computational cost of error enumeration, and (2) the large number of hash function labels required. Addressing these two issues is critical for further advancing the performance of hybrid dual attacks leveraging Meet-LWE.
In addition, a broader question concerning the effectiveness and reliability of (hybrid) dual attacks was raised by Ducas-Pulles [13] in 2023. They argued that the analysis of these attacks relies on unverified heuristics, particularly

the independence heuristic, and lacks sufficient theoretical and experimental justification. More importantly, they showed that some conclusions derived from these heuristics contradict unconditional lattice-theoretic results. This motivates a careful theoretical and empirical study to determine whether the independence assumptions hold for typical FHE parameter regimes under hybrid dual attacks.

1.1 Our Contribution
In this paper, we revisit the Meet-LWE attack and propose a more effective hybrid dual attack for LWE with sparse ternary secrets. Notably, we prove that our attack does not rely on the debated independence heuristic. Our contributions are three-fold.
(1) Improved Understanding of Meet-LWE and Its Variants. We thoroughly revisit Meet-LWE and two of its recent enhancements: the new list construction and hash function techniques (Sections 3 and 4). In particular, we simplify the analysis of the list construction method introduced in [20] by adapting it to the pure Meet-LWE setting, and we develop a more theoretically rigorous method for parameter selection that removes the previous reliance on empirical heuristics. We then perform an in-depth comparison of four Meet-LWE variants obtained by combining different list constructions and hash functions. Our analysis reveals several key insights. Surprisingly, while the new list construction from [20] successfully eliminates error enumeration, it frequently results in worse performance across a wide parameter range due to a fundamental structural bottleneck. In contrast, the new randomized hash function from [31, 20] consistently outperforms Odlyzko's classic hash function [21, 34] by reducing the large number of hash labels at a minor repetition cost. This advantage becomes particularly pronounced with large error distributions, which are typical in hybrid dual attacks. When used together, these two techniques complement each other effectively: the hash function mitigates the main bottleneck of the list construction, while the list construction amplifies the hash function's impact. As a result, their combination achieves the best overall performance in most practical parameter regimes, though some regions still leave room for further optimization. Contrary to the claims of [24], our findings indicate that it is the new list construction, rather than the new hash function, that primarily resolves the error enumeration issue. The hash function instead addresses the amplified label explosion problem. We believe that our detailed analysis of these techniques contributes to a deeper understanding of how these techniques work in the complex scenarios of MITM attack and hybrid dual attack, and lays the groundwork for further improvements.
(2) A More Effective Hybrid Dual Attack. In Section 5, we propose an improved hybrid dual attack based on the Bi-Lu-Luo-Wang (BLLW) algorithm [5], equipped with the above two new techniques. Based on our previous analysis, this change helps to overcome the two main drawbacks of [5]. In addition, regarding the hypothesis testing, we replace the trivial decoder used in previous MITM-accelerated hybrid dual attacks [10, 5] by a better hypothesis testing algorithm referred to as Simple-decoder in [25]. 1.2 Related Work
MITM Attack. Odlyzko's [21] and Howgrave-Graham's [34] MITM attacks are two basic forms of meet-in-the-middle with different splitting methods of the secret: As = A1s1 + A2s2 vs As = A(s1 + s2). Odlyzko's algorithm runs in time O S0.5 , where S is the search space of s and Howgrave-Graham's is worse than that. In 2021, May [31] proposed a new MITM attack named MeetLWE on sparse ternary LWE, reduces the complexity to O S0.25 . This work is followed up by a series of improvements [15, 16, 20, 23, 24, 28] and among them,

Kirshanova-May [24] and Hhan-Kim-Lee-Son [20] independently tried to remove the two auxiliary overheads by using similar techniques and Hhan-Kim-LeeSon [20] combined the improved Meet-LWE into the hybrid primal attack.
Hybrid Dual Attack. The first hybrid dual attack was introduced by Albrecht [2] in 2017. After that, this type of attacks evolves rapidly and has become the most efficient bit-security estimation algorithm of LWE. In 2020, EspitauJoux-Kharchenko [14] introduced a FFT-type distinguisher and an efficient matrix multiplication method to accelerate guessing small secrets. Bi-Lu-Luo-WangZhang [6] further generalized the matrix multiplication method for LWE with arbitrary secrets, and showed that hybrid dual attack always outperforms the standalone dual attack for most cryptographic use cases regardless of the secret distribution. In 2021, Guo-Johansson [17] gave a hybrid dual attack by introducing a FFT distinguisher and a two-step lattice-reduction which is followed quickly by an independent report of MATZOV [30], which further improves the attack by combining it with the modulus switching technique. Two follow up works by Albrecht-Shen [3] and Carrier-Shen-Tillich [8] improve this attack by using quantum algorithm and coding theory, respectively.
For LWE with FHE-type parameters, another branch of hybrid dual attacks using MITM techniques further improves the efficiency. Cheon-Hhan-Hong-Son [10] introduced a hybrid dual attack for LWE with FHE-type parameters by using Howgrave-Graham's MITM attack [34], which significantly improves the performance of the original hybrid dual attack by Albrecht [2]. After that, BiLu-Luo-Wang [5] continue to improve this type of attack by replacing HowgraveGraham's MITM attack with Meet-LWE, a more efficient MITM attack proposed by May [31].
Hybrid Primal Attack. The hybrid primal attack on LWE was first presented by Wunderer [42], which combines the primal attack with HowgraveGraham's MITM attack [34]. This attack works for LWE with arbitrary secret and error. Afterwards, Son-Cheon [40] showed that when focusing on LWE with FHE-type parameters, Wunderer's hybrid primal attack can be further improved because of the structure of the sparse ternary secret. Recently, Hhan-Kim-LeeSon [20] proposed a new hybrid primal attack on FHE that combines primal attack and a generalization of May's Meet-LWE attack [31] using a new list construction.

Then we revisit two recent techniques that enhance Meet-LWE from two perspectives: hash function and list construction (Section 3.2 and 3.3, respectively). In particular, for the list construction technique originally introduced in the context of a hybrid primal attack [20], we present a simplified analysis tailored to the pure Meet-LWE setting. Our contribution includes a more theoretically grounded method for parameter selection, which avoids reliance on empirical heuristics and improves the general applicability of the approach.

3.3 List Construction

Next we revisit a recent list construction technique designed to eliminate the error enumeration. The challenge of removing error enumeration was first posed as an open problem by May [31]. In response, Kirshanova-May [24] introduced a solution that combined a new list construction with a randomized hash function. However, as later noted in [20, Appendix A], their success probability analysis contains a flaw. When corrected, the resulting algorithm becomes impractical.
To address this, Hhan-Kim-Lee-Son [20] proposed a new list construction that also avoids error enumeration and incorporated it into the hybrid primal attack framework. However, due to the complexity introduced by the hybrid setting, their analysis is somewhat intricate and relies on empirical methods for parameter selection.

4 Comparison of Meet-LWE Variants
In the previous section, we revisited two recently proposed techniques aimed at addressing the primary bottlenecks of Meet-LWE, namely, error enumeration and hash label explosion. While both techniques are designed to improve the efficiency of Meet-LWE, their actual effectiveness--whether they offer consistent

An Improved Hybrid Dual Attack on LWE

15

gains and under what conditions--remains incompletely understood, particularly within the pure MITM setting. In this section, we conduct a systematic and indepth comparison of these techniques, both individually and in combination, within the Meet-LWE framework. Our analysis reveals that their performance exhibits subtler and more complex behavior than initially expected. In particular, we highlight the following insights.
­ List Construction (Const.2): Somewhat surprisingly, the new list construction (Const.2) often results in worse performance across a wide range of parameter settings. This is due to a subtle but fundamental structural bottleneck,7 which we analyze in detail.
­ Hash Function (Hash2): As expected, the new hash function Hash2 consistently outperforms Hash1, with its advantage becoming more pronounced when the error distribution is large.
­ Combined Use: When used together, Hash2 and Const.2 complement each other: Hash2 mitigates the primary bottleneck of Const.2, while Const.2 amplifies the benefits of Hash2. This combination achieves better overall performance in most cases, though there remain parameter regimes where further improvements are needed.
We hope these insights clarify the nuanced behavior of these techniques and also provide a foundation for future improvements to Meet-LWE and its variants.
To facilitate a structured comparison, we define four Meet-LWE variants based on the choice of list construction and hash function: MITM1 is the original Meet-LWE; H1C2 replaces Const.1 by Const.2; H2C1 replaces Hash1 by Hash2, and MITM2 combines both changes. Since these attacks involve many interdependent parameters and exhibit complex interactions, we begin in Section 4.1 by analyzing the dependencies among key parameters. Building on this, Section 4.2 compares the four variants under three representative parameter settings, providing insights into their relative strengths and limitations. Finally, Section 4.3 offers a broader comparison across a wide parameter range.

5 Our New Hybrid Dual Attack
In this section we present our new hybrid dual attack by combining dual attack and MITM2 defined in Section 3. As usual, our new attack has two stages: reduction-stage and guessing-stage. The reduction-stage is the same as other hybrid dual attacks, which outputs a new instance with a smaller dimension, a larger error, and also a different secret distribution. Then in the guessing-stage we apply MITM2 with the following two modifications to solve this new instance. In order to cope with the different secret distribution, we analyze the choice of the parameters for the list construction and the corresponding success probability. For the larger error, we replace the original trivial distinguish method in the last hypothesis testing step of MITM2 by a better decoder from LaarhovenWalter [25]. We present the reduction-stage in Section 5.1 and the guessingstage highlighting the two modifications in Section 5.2, and then summarize our algorithm and its complexity in Section 5.3.

6 Clarify the Distinguishing Issues

Recently, Ducas-Pulles [13] identified a contradiction in the heuristic analysis

used in hybrid dual attacks for distinguishing BDD (Bounded Distance Decod-

ing) instance from uniform ones. The issue mainly arises from the independence

heuristic, which assumes that all the individual scores, given by dual vectors, are

mutually independent. In addition, when searching for a BDD instance among

many uniform ones, it becomes likely that some uniform instances may lie closer

to the lattice than the correct BDD target when the number of uniform instances

is large. These concerns have ignited a surge of interest in better understanding

and analyzing (hybrid) dual attacks [41, 37, 38, 12].

In this section, we clarify that our hybrid dual attacks targeting FHE schemes

are not affected by the above two issues. The main reason is that FHE schemes

typically

have

small

ratios

 q

and

w n

,

which

allows

for

simple

instances

for

hy-

pothesis testing and a small number of hypothesis testing instances to be tested,

respectively. Since the BDD target associated with correct guessing is easily dis-

tinguished, this in turn makes it possible to perform hypothesis testing with very

few short vectors. This number is typically smaller than the lattice dimension,

which means the vectors can be chosen to be linearly independent, thus satisfying

the independence heuristic and avoiding the two aforementioned issues.

Next we clarify these issues from three perspectives. First we show that a uniform candidate associated with a wrong guessed partial secret s1 is farther away from the lattice than the correct one with high probability. Then we show that for FHE-type parameters, the number of short vectors used for the hypothesis testing is smaller than the lattice dimension, ensuring their linear independence. At last, we experimentally verify that, for these parameters, the hypothesis testing succeeds with probability close to 1 when using multiple linearly independent short vectors.

To assess the performance of our new attack on practical schemes, we compare ours with previous hybrid attacks on a series of parameters of CKKS [11] (which is a leading scheme widely used in many open-source implementations of homomorphic encryption) and a new accelerated BGV schemes in [29]. The bit-security estimation results are given in Table 4. For all schemes, we use their original costmodels of BKZ to ensure a fair comparison.
As is shown in Table 4, for all cases of CKKS [11], our attack works the best, and our attack greatly reduces the estimation results by 17 to 24 bits under BKZ-coreSVP-model. Although our attack does not invalidate any scheme (the claimed security is 80 bits), it is still noteworthy that for some schemes the results estimated by using our attack is already very close to 80 bits.
For schemes in [29], our new attack significantly reduces the bit-security for all schemes, placing each one clearly below the claimed security-level, with the largest discrepancy being 18 bits. Therefore, all schemes in [29] are invalid under these findings and will need re-parameterization.
In Appendix A, we present a comprehensive comparison of our new attack with the previous hybrid dual attacks (BLLWZ [6], CHHS [10], and BLLW [5]) using three typical cases from [5], where the improvements of our attack is 11-34 bits. We also provide a comparison with the latest hybrid primal attack from [20]. Our attack is on par with it, less than 3 bits away from each other.
8 Conclusion
In this work, we conducted a comprehensive analysis and comparison of four variants of Meet-LWE, utilizing different list constructions and hash functions.
